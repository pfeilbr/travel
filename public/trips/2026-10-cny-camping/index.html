Gotchas

  • Fees: ReserveAmerica rates exclude the reservation fee ($7.25 for the first night) and the park vehicle fee. Same-day reservations close at 3 pm. Walk-ins are allowed for campsites only.
  • Green Lakes cabins: the park website says 2-night minimum, but ReserveAmerica priced cabin #001 at "Use Fees (1 night) $115.50" with no minimum-stay warning. 1 night looks bookable; confirm at checkout.
  • Sampson: the park cottages have a 2-night minimum from Sep 10 to Oct 12, so the 3 listed at $300 probably won't book for 1 night. Seneca Lake Resorts (Campspot) has nothing for 1 night, but 31 cabins are open for Oct 4–6 (2 nights) at about $172–194/night. Camping closes Oct 11.
  • Watkins Glen: no cabins available (they also have a 3-night minimum). Weekends only after Sep 8; Sunday night still showed open sites. Closes Oct 11.
  • Glimmerglass: the park lists a 2-night minimum, but a 1-night search still returned 21 sites. Closes Oct 12.
  • Closing dates on ReserveAmerica: Keuka, Nicks Lake, Sampson and Watkins Glen Oct 11; Glimmerglass Oct 12; Selkirk Shores Oct 24; Treman Nov 7; Moreau Nov 28.
  • Firelight: only tents 16–19 have electric heaters, and early-October nights can be in the 40s, so ask for one of those. Dogs cost $50 extra (non-electric tents only). Late checkout costs $50.

Top 3

  1. Robert H. Treman (details): the cheapest cabin of any option ($80.50, 6 open), waterfall gorge hike, 10 minutes from Ithaca restaurants and bars. Firelight is next door if you'd rather glamp.
  2. Green Lakes: the closest at ~45 min, 3 cabins open, good biking and trout fishing, with Syracuse restaurants nearby. Easiest for a Sunday-to-Monday trip.
  3. Selkirk Shores: 1 cabin left ($128), Lake Ontario sunsets, and the Salmon River run is at its peak, which makes it the best fishing pick.

Spotlight: Robert H. Treman cabins

Rechecked live Thu Oct 1: 6 cabins are open for Sun Oct 4, 1 night (#003, #004, #009, #010, #011, #012), each $80.50 + $7.25 reservation fee. The site page quotes "Use Fees (1 night)" with a "Book these Dates" button, so 1 night is bookable. All 6 are identical. #003 and #012 are ADA accessible.

OutsideOpen porch, fire pit, grill, picnic table. Full shade. Gravel drive-in for up to 2 vehicles, 20 ft max.
BringSheets or sleeping bags, pillows, towels, cookware and utensils, water jugs, and a camp stove if you want to cook beyond the grill.

Nearby in Ithaca

Drive times from the park are approximate. Mondays are slow in Ithaca, and many restaurants close, so plan the big dinner for Sunday.

  • In the park: the Gorge Trail runs 1.5 miles through Enfield Glen past 12 waterfalls, including 115-ft Lucifer Falls. It's a good Monday-morning hike before the 11 am check-out. The trail is seasonal, so check for alerts before you go.
  • Gorges nearby: Buttermilk Falls SP is about 5 min away. Ithaca Falls is in town. Taughannock Falls (215 ft) is about 25 min north.
  • Biking: the Cayuga Waterfront Trail is 6 flat, paved miles linking Stewart Park, the Farmers Market and Cass Park. The Black Diamond Trail is 8.4 miles of stone dust from Cass Park/Allan H. Treman SP to Taughannock Falls. Bring your bikes; I couldn't confirm October hours for local rentals.
  • Fishing: Cayuga Lake shore access at Allan H. Treman State Marine Park and Stewart Park, about 15 min away. A NY fishing license is required.
  • Sunday daytime: the Ithaca Farmers Market at Steamboat Landing runs Sundays 10 am–3 pm in season. That's a natural stop on the drive in, but confirm Oct 4 at ithacamarket.com. The Apple Harvest Festival already happened (Sep 25–27).
  • Sunday dinner and drinks: Moosewood, the vegetarian landmark, serves Sundays 5–9 pm. Monks on the Commons is open Sundays until 11 pm. Ithaca Beer Co.'s taproom is about 5 min from the park (check hours). The Ithaca Commons is about 10–15 min away for an after-dinner walk.
  • Monday on the way home: a quick stop at Taughannock or Buttermilk Falls. Cayuga Lake wineries along Route 89 are on the way north, but check Monday hours.
